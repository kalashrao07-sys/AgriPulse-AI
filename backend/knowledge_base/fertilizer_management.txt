FERTILIZER AND NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT FOR INDIAN AGRICULTURE

IMPORTANCE OF SOIL TESTING
Always test soil before applying fertilizers.
Test parameters: pH, N, P, K, organic carbon, micronutrients.
Recommended labs: Soil Testing Labs at Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or State Agriculture Department.
Frequency: Once every 2-3 years or before every major crop.

MAJOR NUTRIENTS AND THEIR ROLES

Nitrogen (N)
Role: Promotes vegetative growth, leaf development, protein synthesis.
Deficiency symptoms: Yellowing of older/lower leaves (chlorosis), stunted growth.
Excess symptoms: Dark green lush growth, delayed flowering, increased disease susceptibility.
Sources: Urea (46% N), DAP (18% N), Ammonium Sulphate (21% N)
Application: Split applications are most efficient. Apply in 3 splits (Basal, 30 days, 60 days).

Phosphorus (P)
Role: Root development, flowering, energy transfer (ATP), seed formation.
Deficiency symptoms: Purple/reddish coloration of leaves and stems, poor root development.
Excess: Can lock out micronutrients. Apply based on soil test.
Sources: DAP (46% P2O5), SSP (16% P2O5), Rock Phosphate
Application: Apply mostly as basal dose (at planting). Less mobile in soil.

Potassium (K)
Role: Fruit quality, disease resistance, water regulation, enzyme activation.
Deficiency symptoms: Scorching/browning of leaf margins, poor fruit quality.
Sources: MOP/Muriate of Potash (60% K2O), SOP/Sulphate of Potash (50% K2O)
Application: Apply as basal + one top dressing at fruiting stage.

SECONDARY AND MICRONUTRIENTS

Calcium (Ca)
Essential for cell wall development and fruit quality.
Deficiency in tomato causes Blossom End Rot (BER) — dark leathery patch at fruit bottom.
Sources: Calcium Nitrate (foliar), Lime (soil application).
Application: Spray Calcium Nitrate @ 1% at fruiting stage.

Magnesium (Mg)
Deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between leaf veins while veins stay green).
Sources: Magnesium Sulphate (Epsom Salt) @ 10g/L foliar spray.

Zinc (Zn)
Deficiency: Small leaves, short internodes, brown/bronze spots.
Sources: Zinc Sulphate @ 25kg/ha soil application, or foliar @ 5g/L.

Boron (B)
Deficiency in tomato: Cracked fruits, tip burn, hollow stems.
Sources: Borax @ 1g/L foliar spray.

FERTILIZER SCHEDULE FOR TOMATO (per acre)
At transplanting: FYM 4 tonnes + DAP 40kg + MOP 15kg
At 30 days: Urea 10kg
At 45 days (flowering): Foliar 19:19:19 NPK @ 5g/L + Calcium Nitrate @ 10g/L
At 60 days (fruiting): MOP 10kg + Foliar Calcium Nitrate @ 10g/L
At 75 days: Foliar 0:52:34 (Mono Potassium Phosphate) @ 5g/L

FERTILIZER SCHEDULE FOR WHEAT (per acre)
Basal: DAP 50kg + MOP 10kg
At 25 days (crown root initiation): Urea 25kg
At 45 days (tillering): Urea 25kg
Foliar at grain fill: Urea @ 1% solution

ORGANIC FARMING OPTIONS
Vermicompost: 2-3 tonnes/acre as basal. Improves soil health.
Bio-fertilizers:
- Rhizobium (for legumes): Fixes atmospheric nitrogen
- PSB (Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria): Makes phosphorus available
- Azotobacter: Free-living nitrogen fixation
Neem cake: 100kg/acre. Repels nematodes, adds nutrients.
Panchagavya: 3% spray. Improves plant immunity and microbial activity.

FERTIGATION THROUGH DRIP SYSTEM
Advantages: Precise placement, reduces losses, saves fertilizer 20-30%.
Water-soluble fertilizers: Urea, Potassium Nitrate (13:0:45), Potassium Sulphate, Calcium Nitrate.
Do not mix Calcium with Phosphate or Sulfate — forms precipitate.
EC (Electrical Conductivity) of fertigation solution: 1.5-2.5 mS/cm for most vegetables.

SOIL pH MANAGEMENT
Acidic soil (pH < 6): Apply agricultural lime (Calcium Carbonate) @ 200-400 kg/acre.
Alkaline soil (pH > 7.5): Apply Gypsum @ 100-200 kg/acre. Use ammoniacal fertilizers.
Most vegetables grow best at pH 6.0-7.0.
