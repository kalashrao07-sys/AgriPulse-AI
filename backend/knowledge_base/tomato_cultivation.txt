TOMATO CULTIVATION — COMPLETE GUIDE FOR INDIAN FARMERS

CROP OVERVIEW
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is one of the most important vegetable crops in India.
It is grown throughout the year in Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Tamil Nadu.
Optimal growing temperature: 20°C to 27°C. Cannot tolerate frost.

SOIL REQUIREMENTS
Best soil: Well-drained sandy loam or loam soil.
pH range: 6.0 to 7.0 (slightly acidic to neutral).
Avoid waterlogged soils — causes root rot.
Add organic matter (FYM) to improve soil structure.

GROWTH STAGES AND DURATION
1. Seedling stage: 0-15 days — Germination and early growth in nursery.
2. Transplanting: Day 25-30 — Transplant to main field when seedlings are 15-20cm tall.
3. Vegetative stage: 15-30 days after transplanting — Rapid leaf and stem growth.
4. Flowering stage: 30-60 days after transplanting — First flowers appear. Critical irrigation stage.
5. Fruiting stage: 60-90 days — Green fruits develop and size up.
6. Harvest: 90-120 days — Fruits ripen. Harvest every 3-4 days.

FERTILIZER SCHEDULE
Basal application (at transplanting):
- Farm Yard Manure (FYM): 25 tonnes/hectare
- DAP (Di-ammonium Phosphate): 250 kg/ha
- MOP (Muriate of Potash): 50 kg/ha

Top dressing:
- At 30 days: Urea 50 kg/ha
- At 45 days (flowering): 19:19:19 NPK foliar @ 5g/L
- At 60 days (fruiting): MOP 50 kg/ha + Calcium Nitrate foliar

NPK requirement: 120:60:60 kg/ha (N:P:K)

IRRIGATION MANAGEMENT
Critical irrigation stages:
- Transplanting: Immediate light irrigation
- Vegetative: Every 5-7 days
- Flowering: Every 4-5 days (most critical — do not stress)
- Fruiting: Every 5-6 days
- Harvest: Reduce irrigation

Water requirement: 400-600mm per season.
Drip irrigation efficiency: 90-95% water use efficiency.
Drip dosing: 2-3 liters/plant/day at peak season.

SOIL MOISTURE GUIDELINES
Optimal: 50-70% field capacity
Minimum: 40% field capacity (irrigate below this)
Maximum: 80% (risk of root rot and disease above this)

When rain probability is above 60%, delay irrigation.
When soil moisture is above 65%, delay irrigation.
When soil moisture is below 40%, irrigate immediately.

STAKING AND TRAINING
Stake plants at 30-35 days using bamboo/wooden stakes 1.5m tall.
Tie loosely with soft cloth or rope. Remove suckers for single-stem training.
Pruning lower leaves improves air circulation and reduces disease.

COMMON VARIETIES IN KARNATAKA
Arka Vikas: Indeterminate, good for fresh market.
Arka Rakshak: Resistant to ToLCV virus, bacterial wilt.
NS 585: High yield hybrid.
Rashmi: Suitable for processing.
Naveen: Semi-determinate, good shelf life.
