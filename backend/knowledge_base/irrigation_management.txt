IRRIGATION AND WATER MANAGEMENT FOR INDIAN FARMERS

IMPORTANCE OF IRRIGATION MANAGEMENT
Water is the most critical input for crop production. Improper irrigation leads to:
- Over-irrigation: Root rot, nutrient leaching, disease outbreaks, waterlogging
- Under-irrigation: Wilting, yield reduction, poor fruit quality

SOIL MOISTURE AND IRRIGATION SCHEDULING

Field Capacity (FC): Maximum water soil can hold after drainage — 100% FC.
Permanent Wilting Point (PWP): Minimum moisture at which plants can survive.
Available Water Capacity (AWC) = FC - PWP

Optimal crop growth: 50-80% of field capacity maintained.
Irrigation trigger: When soil moisture falls below 50% AWC.
Irrigation stop: When soil moisture reaches 80% AWC.

WHEN TO DELAY IRRIGATION
1. Rain is expected (probability > 60%) in next 24-48 hours
2. Soil moisture is above 65-70%
3. Recent heavy rainfall received
4. Crop showing no water stress signs
5. Cloudy cool weather (low evapotranspiration)

WHEN TO IRRIGATE
1. Soil moisture below 40-45%
2. Morning wilting observed in plants
3. No rain expected for 7+ days
4. High temperature (above 35°C) and low humidity
5. Critical crop stages: flowering, fruit fill

IRRIGATION METHODS COMPARISON
Flood Irrigation: Low cost, high water use (1000-1500mm/season). Risk of waterlogging.
Furrow Irrigation: Moderate efficiency. Good for row crops.
Sprinkler Irrigation: 70-80% efficiency. Good for field crops.
Drip Irrigation: 90-95% efficiency. Best for vegetables and orchards. Reduces disease.

DRIP IRRIGATION MANAGEMENT
Installation: Laterals at 60cm spacing for tomato. Emitters at 30-45cm intervals.
Flow rate: 2-4 liters/hour per emitter depending on crop stage.
Daily operation: Run drip for 1-3 hours depending on soil moisture and temperature.
Fertigation: Inject soluble fertilizers through drip system.
Maintenance: Flush laterals weekly. Check emitters for clogging monthly.

CROP WATER REQUIREMENTS (mm/week)
Tomato: Seedling 10-15mm, Vegetative 20-25mm, Flowering 35-40mm, Fruiting 25-30mm
Rice: 50-80mm/week (standing water maintained)
Wheat: 20-30mm/week
Maize: 25-35mm/week
Cotton: 30-40mm/week

RAINFALL AND IRRIGATION
Karnataka receives:
- Kharif season (June-September): 600-900mm rainfall
- Rabi season (October-February): 100-200mm rainfall

Use rainwater harvesting ponds during monsoon.
Check IMD (India Meteorological Department) forecasts for planning.

SOIL MOISTURE MONITORING
Feel method:
- Dry: Sandy feel, no moist appearance → Below 25% FC → Irrigate immediately
- Moist: Forms a ball when squeezed → 50-70% FC → Good moisture
- Wet: Water squeezes out easily → Above 80% FC → Delay irrigation
- Saturated: Standing water → Waterlogged → Stop all irrigation

IMPACT OF WEATHER ON IRRIGATION
High temperature (>35°C): Increase irrigation frequency by 20-30%.
Low humidity (<40%): Evapotranspiration is high — more irrigation needed.
Wind speed >15km/h: Increases water loss — irrigate in morning to reduce evaporation.
Rain probability >70%: Delay irrigation. Use forecasted rainfall.

WATER CONSERVATION TIPS
1. Mulching (straw or plastic): Reduces soil moisture evaporation by 30-40%.
2. Drip over flood: Saves 40-50% water.
3. Irrigate in morning: Reduces evaporation losses.
4. Check soil before irrigating: Never irrigate on schedule alone — check moisture.
5. Avoid waterlogging: Ensures oxygen availability for roots.
