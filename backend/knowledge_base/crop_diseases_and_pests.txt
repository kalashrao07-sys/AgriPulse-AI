CROP DISEASES AND PEST MANAGEMENT — VEGETABLES AND FIELD CROPS

FUNGAL DISEASES

Early Blight (Alternaria solani)
Crops affected: Tomato, Potato, Brinjal
Symptoms: Dark brown spots with concentric rings (target-board pattern) on lower/older leaves. Yellow halo around spots.
Favorable conditions: Warm temperature (24-29°C), high humidity, rainy weather.
Management:
- Remove and destroy infected leaves
- Apply Mancozeb 75WP @ 2g/L water
- Apply Copper Oxychloride 50WP @ 3g/L
- Apply Chlorothalonil @ 2g/L
- Spray at 10-14 day intervals
Prevention: Avoid overhead irrigation. Maintain plant spacing. Crop rotation for 2-3 years. Use resistant varieties.

Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
Crops affected: Tomato, Potato
Symptoms: Water-soaked lesions on leaves turning brown-black. White cottony growth on leaf underside in humid conditions.
Very destructive — can destroy crop within a week.
Management:
- Apply Metalaxyl + Mancozeb @ 2.5g/L
- Apply Cymoxanil + Mancozeb @ 2.5g/L
- Apply preventively before disease appearance in humid seasons
Prevention: Plant resistant varieties. Avoid leaf wetness. Remove infected plants immediately.

Powdery Mildew
Crops affected: Tomato, Cucumber, Bottle gourd, Wheat
Symptoms: White powdery coating on leaf surface. Leaves curl and die.
Management:
- Sulfur 80WP @ 3g/L water
- Hexaconazole @ 1ml/L
- Trifloxystrobin @ 0.5g/L
Prevention: Improve air circulation. Avoid excess nitrogen.

Damping Off (Pythium, Rhizoctonia)
Crops affected: Seedlings of all crops
Symptoms: Seedlings collapse at soil level. Soft stem rot at ground level.
Management:
- Treat seeds with Thiram or Captan @ 3g/kg seed
- Drench soil with Copper Oxychloride @ 3g/L
Prevention: Avoid overwatering nursery. Use sterile growing media.

BACTERIAL DISEASES

Bacterial Wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)
Crops affected: Tomato, Brinjal, Potato, Chilli
Symptoms: Sudden wilting of entire plant without yellowing. Brown vascular tissue when stem is cut.
No effective chemical control. Soil-borne pathogen.
Management:
- No curative spray available
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately
- Do not compost infected material
- Soil solarization (cover with clear plastic for 4-6 weeks)
- Crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops for 3-4 years
- Use resistant varieties where available (Arka Rakshak for tomato)
Prevention: Avoid injury to roots. Use certified disease-free transplants.

INSECT PESTS

Leaf Miner (Liriomyza spp.)
Crops affected: Tomato, Beans, Peas
Symptoms: Irregular serpentine mines/tunnels on leaf surface. Leaves turn yellow.
Management:
- Apply Spinosad @ 0.2ml/L water
- Apply Abamectin @ 0.5ml/L
- Apply Cyromazine @ 0.6g/L
- Use yellow sticky traps @ 10/acre for monitoring
Prevention: Destroy infected leaves. Parasitic wasps (natural enemies) — avoid broad-spectrum sprays.

Aphids (Aphis gossypii, Myzus persicae)
Crops affected: Most vegetable crops
Symptoms: Small green, yellow, or black insects clustered on shoot tips. Sticky honeydew. Sooty mold. Virus transmission.
Management:
- Imidacloprid @ 0.3ml/L
- Thiamethoxam @ 0.4g/L
- Neem oil @ 3ml/L (organic option)
- Dimethoate @ 1.5ml/L
Prevention: Reflective silver mulch repels aphids. Encourage ladybird beetles.

Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)
Crops affected: Tomato (important vector of ToLCV virus), Cotton, Beans
Symptoms: Tiny white flies fly when plant is disturbed. Yellow stippled leaves. Virus transmission.
Management:
- Yellow sticky traps @ 15/acre
- Imidacloprid @ 0.3ml/L (systemic)
- Spinosad @ 0.2ml/L
- Neem oil @ 5ml/L
Prevention: Remove infected plants. Avoid continuous tomato cropping.

Fruit Borer (Helicoverpa armigera)
Crops affected: Tomato, Cotton, Chickpea
Symptoms: Circular entry holes in fruits. Larvae found inside fruits.
Management:
- Pheromone traps @ 5/acre for monitoring
- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) @ 1g/L (biological)
- Spinosad @ 0.2ml/L
- Emamectin benzoate @ 0.4g/L
Prevention: Destroy infected fruits. Crop rotation.

INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM) PRINCIPLES
1. Regular scouting: Visit field twice weekly. Record pest and disease observations.
2. Economic threshold: Spray only when pest population crosses threshold.
3. Biological control: Use natural enemies — Trichogramma cards, parasitic wasps.
4. Cultural control: Crop rotation, resistant varieties, proper spacing, sanitation.
5. Chemical control: Last resort. Rotate insecticide groups to prevent resistance.
6. Spray timing: Early morning or evening. Avoid spraying before rain.
7. Safety: Use PPE when applying chemicals. Follow label instructions.
